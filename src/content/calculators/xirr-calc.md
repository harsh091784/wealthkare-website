### What is it?
XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return) calculates your true annualized return when investments happen at irregular intervals — like SIPs, additional purchases, and partial withdrawals. It is the single most accurate measure of your actual mutual fund returns.

### The Formula
XIRR solves for the rate at which the net present value of all your cash flows equals zero, accounting for the exact date of each transaction.

### Why it Matters
Your mutual fund statement shows XIRR because simple returns are misleading for SIPs. If you invested ₹10,000 monthly for 3 years and your corpus shows 20% absolute gain, your XIRR could be anywhere from 11% to 14% depending on when the market moved. XIRR tells you the truth.

### How to Use it
Add each investment as a negative amount with its date. Add your current portfolio value (or redemption) as a positive amount with today's date. The result is your real annualized return.

### Assumptions
Requires accurate dates. This is the same methodology used in your official fund statements.
