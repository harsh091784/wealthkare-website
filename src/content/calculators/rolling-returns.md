### What is it?
Rolling returns measure how an investment performed across every possible holding period of a fixed length — every 3-year window, for example — rather than just point-to-point returns.

### The Formula
For each start date in the sample, calculate the CAGR over the rolling window (1, 3, 5, or 7 years), then aggregate across all windows.

### Why it Matters
Point-to-point returns can be deeply misleading — they depend entirely on the start and end dates chosen. Rolling returns reveal consistency: a fund averaging 12% with rolling returns between 8% and 16% is far more dependable than one averaging 12% with a range of -5% to 30%.

### How to Use it
Choose a fund category and a rolling period. Look at the minimum rolling return — that's what you could have earned in the worst-case entry timing.

### Assumptions
Sample data is illustrative only. Actual fund performance varies. Longer rolling periods show more stability in equity categories.
