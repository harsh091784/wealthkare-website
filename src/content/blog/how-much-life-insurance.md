Most people buy life insurance the same way they buy most things — by picking a number that sounds substantial. "₹1 crore" feels like a lot. But a sound cover amount isn't about what sounds impressive; it's about what your family would actually need to maintain their life without your income.

The starting point is Human Life Value — the present value of your future income that your family would lose if you weren't there to earn it. This isn't your current salary multiplied by a round number; it's a calculation involving your income, how many years until you'd have retired, expected income growth, and a reasonable discount rate to bring future earnings back to today's value.

For a working professional earning ₹15 lakhs annually with 25 years left until retirement, the income-replacement component alone often runs into several crores — far beyond the "1 crore feels like enough" instinct most people start with. This is the single biggest reason Indians remain chronically underinsured: the emotional comfort of a round number, rather than a calculation grounded in actual need.

Beyond income replacement, a complete cover should account for two more things. First, outstanding liabilities — any home loan, car loan, or other debt your family shouldn't inherit alongside their grief. Second, future one-time obligations your income would have funded — children's education, marriage costs, or other planned expenses that don't disappear just because the income does.

A useful framework: Cover = (Annual income × years of replacement needed) + Outstanding loans + Future family obligations − Existing cover. This produces a number grounded in your actual life, not an arbitrary figure.

It's worth separating this clearly from investment-linked insurance products. Term insurance — pure protection with no investment component — is almost always the most cost-efficient way to get adequate cover, because every rupee of premium goes toward the death benefit rather than being split with fund management. A ₹2 crore term cover for a healthy 30-year-old costs a fraction of what an equivalent investment-linked policy would charge for the same protection.

Cover needs aren't static either. A newly married 28-year-old with no children needs less cover than the same person at 35 with two children and a home loan. Reviewing your cover every few years, or after major life events — marriage, a child, a new loan — keeps your protection aligned with your actual obligations rather than a number chosen once and forgotten.

The goal of life insurance isn't to leave behind a windfall. It's to ensure your absence doesn't force your family into financial hardship on top of everything else they're already facing.
