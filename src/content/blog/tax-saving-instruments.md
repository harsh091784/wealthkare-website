Section 80C offers a ₹1.5 lakh annual deduction across a wide menu of instruments — but the menu itself creates a common problem: investors often pick based on what's familiar or aggressively marketed, rather than what actually fits their goals and risk tolerance.

ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme) mutual funds offer the shortest lock-in among 80C options at 3 years, invest in equity markets, and historically offer the highest growth potential among 80C instruments — at the cost of market-linked volatility with no guaranteed return.

PPF (Public Provident Fund) sits at the opposite end — a 15-year lock-in, government-guaranteed fixed interest rate, and complete tax exemption on both interest and maturity (full EEE status). It offers certainty with modest growth, functioning as the safe, guaranteed core of an 80C portfolio.

NPS (National Pension System), while primarily a retirement vehicle, offers 80C eligibility for contributions up to ₹1.5 lakhs, plus a valuable additional ₹50,000 deduction under 80CCD(1B) that sits outside the standard 80C limit entirely — making it one of the few ways to extend total tax-saving deduction beyond ₹1.5 lakhs.

Tax-saving fixed deposits offer 80C eligibility with a mandatory 5-year lock-in, providing FD-like safety and predictability, though the interest earned is fully taxable — reducing the effective post-tax return meaningfully compared to the fund's headline rate, an important detail often overlooked.

Life insurance premiums (for genuine protection policies, not investment-linked ones) also qualify under 80C, along with EPF contributions that salaried employees are typically already making automatically through payroll — meaning many salaried individuals are unknowingly already using a meaningful portion of their 80C limit before actively choosing anything.

The comparison isn't really about which single instrument is "best" — it's about which combination serves your actual risk tolerance and time horizon. A younger investor with a longer horizon and higher risk tolerance might lean toward ELSS for its growth potential and shorter lock-in. Someone prioritizing absolute certainty might favour PPF despite its longer lock-in. Most sound 80C strategies blend 2-3 instruments rather than concentrating entirely in one — balancing growth potential, guaranteed safety, and liquidity needs across the ₹1.5 lakh limit.

Before choosing where to invest for this year's 80C deduction, it's worth first checking how much of the limit is already being used automatically through EPF contributions and any existing life insurance premiums — many people are further along toward the ₹1.5 lakh cap than they realize, before making any new active investment decision.
