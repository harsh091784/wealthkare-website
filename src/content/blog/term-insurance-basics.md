Term insurance is the simplest, most cost-efficient form of life insurance available — pure protection, with no investment component, paying a defined sum to your family if you pass away during the policy term. Understanding a few core decisions makes choosing the right policy considerably less confusing.

The most important decision is the sum assured — the cover amount. A useful starting framework: multiply your annual income by the number of years remaining until you would have retired, then add any outstanding loans your family shouldn't inherit, then add a buffer for future obligations like children's education or marriage. This produces a figure grounded in actual need, rather than a round number chosen because it sounds substantial.

Policy term is the second key decision — how long the cover lasts. A common approach is matching the term to your working years, ensuring cover remains in place until retirement, when your family's dependence on your income would naturally reduce as other assets have had time to accumulate. Choosing too short a term risks losing cover precisely when family obligations may still be significant.

Riders — optional add-ons to a base policy — deserve individual evaluation rather than blanket acceptance or rejection. A critical illness rider pays out on diagnosis of specified serious illnesses, providing funds when medical costs and lost income can both hit simultaneously. An accidental death benefit rider adds an additional payout specifically for accidental death. A waiver of premium rider continues the cover even if you become unable to pay due to disability. Each adds cost, so selecting riders that address genuine gaps in your existing coverage — rather than adding every available option — keeps the policy efficient.

A frequently overlooked factor is the claim settlement ratio and claim settlement speed of the insurer — publicly available data showing what percentage of claims an insurer actually pays, and how quickly. A policy is only as valuable as the insurer's reliability in honouring it when your family actually needs to claim, making this data worth checking before choosing a provider, not just comparing premium quotes.

Term insurance should be purchased as early as possible for two compounding reasons: premiums are locked in based on age and health at purchase, rising significantly with age and any health conditions that develop later; and a delay of even a few years, while healthy, can mean paying meaningfully more for the same cover, or in the worst case, facing exclusions or higher premiums due to a health condition that emerges in the interim.

It's worth being clear about what term insurance is not: it's not an investment, and comparing its premium unfavourably against investment-linked products misunderstands its purpose. Every rupee of a term premium goes toward pure protection — which is precisely why it's the most cost-efficient way to secure adequate cover, leaving investment decisions to be made separately, through instruments actually designed for growth.
