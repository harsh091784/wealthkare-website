Higher education costs in India have been rising faster than general inflation for over a decade, and international education costs faster still. A course that costs ₹15 lakhs today can realistically cost ₹35-40 lakhs by the time a child born today reaches college age. Planning for this isn't optional — it's arithmetic that needs to start early.

The first step is estimating the actual future cost, not today's cost. Education inflation in India has historically run at 10-12% annually, meaningfully higher than the 6% general inflation most people default to when planning. Using the wrong inflation assumption is the single most common mistake in education planning — it leads parents to under-save by a wide margin without realizing it until the goal is much closer.

Once you have a realistic future target, the strategy should shift with time horizon — a principle sometimes called a "glide path." With 12-15 years until the goal, a portfolio can afford significant equity exposure, since there's enough time to ride out volatility and benefit from higher long-term growth. As the goal approaches — inside 3-5 years — the allocation should progressively shift toward debt and more stable instruments, protecting the accumulated corpus from a market downturn arriving right when the money is needed.

This glide path matters more than people realize. A portfolio that stays 100% equity right up until the fee payment is due is exposed to sequence-of-returns risk — a market fall in year 14 of a 15-year plan can meaningfully dent a corpus with no time left to recover. Shifting to safer instruments as the goal nears removes this risk at the cost of some growth potential, which is the right trade-off that close to a fixed deadline.

Multiple smaller SIPs targeting specific milestones — school fees, undergraduate costs, postgraduate or international study — often work better than one large undifferentiated fund, because each milestone has its own timeline and can be invested accordingly.

It's also worth revisiting the plan periodically, not setting it once and forgetting it. A child's academic direction, choice of field, and whether they pursue education abroad can all shift the actual cost significantly from the original assumption — a plan built when a child is 3 should be revisited by the time they're 10, and again as they approach college age.

Education is one of the few financial goals with a genuinely fixed deadline and no flexibility to postpone. Starting the calculation early — with realistic inflation assumptions — is what separates a comfortable funding position from a stressful scramble for loans when the fee notice arrives.
